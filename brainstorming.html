ARCHIVED 13 · PHOTOSHOP · SCOPE OF CHANGE

Layer Masks — change one part while preserving context

Current framework: primarily a known unknowns interaction candidate. The user already recognizes an unresolved region; the mechanism is decomposition + externalization of scope, making explicit what may change and what should remain fixed while reasoning continues.

Existing interaction

In Photoshop, a user can add a layer mask and edit it to show or hide selected portions of a layer. A mask on an adjustment layer can limit that adjustment to chosen parts of an image, leaving other regions untouched. The mask remains editable.

Possible CAD interaction (hypothesis)

  1. Select the head-support region in the full crawling-trainer assembly.
  2. Indicate what may change during this exploration and which surrounding geometry should remain unchanged.
  3. Explore a replacement head support while keeping the rest of the assembly visible, inspect connections and clearance, and revise the scope if necessary.
  4. Review the proposed change before applying it to the shared design.

What seems interesting: the user defines the boundary of an exploration through a mask that controls where change can occur. A 3D assembly complicates this because local edits may affect connections and adjacent components outside the selected region.

Open question: Should the scope limit actual geometry edits, identify design decisions to preserve, or define where a suggested modification may apply? These are not equivalent and need to be explored separately.

ARCHIVED 14 · FIGMA · CONTEXT OF INSPECTION

Dev Mode — inspect the same design through another working context

2026 framework mapping · Group × Problem-first or Solution-first (candidate): switching working context may reveal missing evaluation criteria or relevant knowledge held by another collaborator, or help a user seek expertise once a gap is already recognized. The candidate mechanism is perspective taking + contextualization.

Existing interaction

Figma provides a dedicated developer-facing inspection and handoff environment. Its Dev Mode includes inspection tools, design-specific information and focus views. Role inference and current reasoning needs remain separate interaction questions for a CAD transfer.

Possible CAD interaction (hypothesis)

  1. Open the same crawling-trainer model and choose the current working context, such as design, engineering, physical therapy or fabrication.
  2. Inspect relevant geometry alongside the knowledge and questions associated with that task: e.g., structural assumptions for engineering or contact and movement requirements for physical therapy.
  3. Switch contexts or inspect information from another discipline without losing the common model or restricting access by role.
  4. Contribute findings to the relevant geometry for the next collaborator.

What seems interesting: different people can approach the same geometry with different questions. The user controls the view, and overlapping requirements remain visible across relevant contexts.

Open question: How can contextual views expose relevant information without concealing dependencies or considerations outside the user's expertise? Task-based views should not become rigid role permissions.
