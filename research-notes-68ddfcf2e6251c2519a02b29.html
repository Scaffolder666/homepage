Interactive Support for Informed Design Reasoning

Design rarely begins with a fully specified problem, stable goals, or a known solution. Designers develop the problem and possible solutions together as they interact with artifacts, constraints, feedback, collaborators, and the world. My research develops interactive design tools that help relevant grounds enter this reasoning while designers retain ownership of their goals and judgments.

How can interactive design tools support informed design reasoning?

01 · What is holding back informed design reasoning?

Design tools make geometry explicit before reasoning is complete

Designers make consequential choices while their understanding is still developing. A choice about an artifact can change what becomes possible later, yet the knowledge needed to judge that choice may emerge only through comparison, expertise, simulation, fabrication, testing, or collaboration. The problem is therefore not simply a lack of information. Relevant grounds may exist but remain outside the designer's current reasoning, or they may not exist until the designer generates new evidence.

Interactive design tools strongly shape this situation. CAD makes geometric state precise and actionable, but precision in the representation does not mean that the reasoning behind a choice is equally settled. A tool can therefore help a designer move forward while still failing to bring the grounds needed to understand what that move means.

Central thesis. Interactive design tools can support more informed design reasoning by making relevant forms of reasoning available around the aspects of a design that designers are currently developing.

02 · Informed design reasoning

What does it mean for design reasoning to be informed?

I call design reasoning informed when relevant grounds enter the designer's judgment while that judgment is being formed. The criterion is not exhaustive information or a successful final artifact. It is whether the designer can reason with grounds that matter to the current design situation before deciding how to move forward.

This definition exposes two questions. First, relevance depends on what aspect of the design the designer is reasoning about. Grounds relevant to an artifact's purpose differ from those relevant to its behavior or physical structure. Second, the same aspect can become better informed through different forms of reasoning. A designer may need to reconsider the current situation, retrieve knowledge, use an analogy, compare alternatives, anticipate consequences, or generate evidence through a prototype.

03 · Deriving the dimensions

Two literature-grounded dimensions

The dimensions come from different bodies of literature because they answer different questions. Design Aspect uses an existing ontology of the design object. Reasoning Support is a synthesis across established design-cognition and design-support traditions. I do not treat the six reasoning-support categories as an existing taxonomy or as an exhaustive ontology of cognition.

Dimension 1 · Design Aspect

Gero and Kannengiesser's Function–Behaviour–Structure framework explicitly defines three classes of variables as different aspects of a design object: Function describes what the object is for, Behavior what it does, and Structure its components and their relationships. I use these three categories as the content dimension of the research space.

Dimension 2 · Reasoning Support

For the second dimension, I group recurring ways that design research describes designers becoming better informed. Hay et al.'s systematic review is useful as a boundary: design cognition contains broader cognitive processes than the six categories below. The purpose here is narrower—identifying forms of reasoning that an interactive design tool can deliberately support.

The separation also clarifies a third level: interaction mechanisms implement reasoning support but are not themselves categories in the space. Prompts and critique may support reflection; search and contextual resurfacing may support retrieval; juxtaposition may support comparison; simulation may support consequence reasoning; fabrication and testing may support prototyping.

05 · Why design to fabrication?

A domain where incomplete reasoning becomes consequential

My current work focuses on design to fabrication because digital choices acquire physical consequences. Geometry, material, assembly, tolerance, fabrication process, and physical behavior become coupled, while relevant expertise can be distributed across designers, engineers, fabricators, domain experts, and users. Fabrication and use also produce evidence that a digital representation cannot fully contain.

This makes design to fabrication a useful testbed for the agenda. The domain exposes when relevant grounds are missing from current reasoning and lets interactive systems intervene through representation, information, comparison, simulation, collaboration, and physical testing.

06 · Three steps toward informed design reasoning

Current work progressively occupies the research space

Reflection

aCADemy · Bringing overlooked considerations into ongoing design

Designers can make a modeling move without considering fabrication implications that become important later. aCADemy introduces situated reflective questions during CAD modeling so these considerations can enter reasoning while the design is still developing. The project demonstrates one way an interactive tool can support reflection without prescribing the designer's goal or decision.

Knowledge retrieval · collaboration

FuzzyCAD · Letting unresolved decisions reach relevant expertise

Some design decisions cannot yet be resolved with the knowledge available to the current designer. FuzzyCAD lets designers represent unresolved geometric decisions and communicate the constraints and uncertainty around them so collaborators with relevant expertise can continue the reasoning. The project extends informed reasoning from an individual interaction to an asynchronous collaborative process.

07 · Research agenda

Building interactive support across the space

The matrix turns the dissertation from a collection of systems into a generative research program. Existing projects occupy only part of the space. Unexplored combinations suggest concrete questions: how analogy can help designers reconsider function, how retrieved expertise can inform behavior, how comparison can expose structural trade-offs, how simulation can make consequences inspectable, and how prototypes can update judgments across all three design aspects.

A second challenge is deciding when and how support should enter the interaction. The goal is not continuous coaching. Future systems can use observable properties of the design situation to make a relevant reasoning opportunity available while leaving the designer in control of whether to engage with it.

A third challenge is distribution. Relevant grounds can reside in another person, artifact, representation, or later physical test. This extends the agenda beyond individual CAD use toward collaborative design, architecture, product design, assistive technology, and other settings where consequential decisions develop under incomplete knowledge.
