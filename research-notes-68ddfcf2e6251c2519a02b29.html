Research agenda · Work in exploration

How might we support design reasoning in CAD?

I study consequential design decisions through their epistemic state: what designers recognize, what they know they do not know, and what relevant knowledge remains unactivated. My current setting is CAD, where decisions become embedded in geometry and fabrication plans. I investigate how interactive tools can surface overlooked decisions, support reasoning through known uncertainties, and elicit knowledge designers already have but have not yet brought into the decision.

01 · Introduction

Introduction

Design is a wicked problem. Goals, constraints, and possible solutions evolve together, so designers cannot fully plan their work in advance. CAD and AI automation are increasingly effective when a designer already knows what needs to be done and has a clear plan for doing it. My research focuses on the less resolved moments: when an important decision has not yet been recognized, when a designer knows that something is unresolved but cannot yet decide, or when relevant knowledge exists but has not been activated in the current situation. I investigate how CAD interactions can invoke cognitive mechanisms that help designers notice, articulate, and reason through these decisions.

02 · Research framework

Epistemic States of Design Decisions

I treat the unit of analysis as a design decision at a particular moment, not a fixed property of a person. The vertical axis asks whether the designer is aware of the decision or knowledge gap. The horizontal axis asks whether the relevant knowledge, evidence, experience, or expertise needed to resolve it is currently available to the designer or team. Together these dimensions describe four epistemic states: unknown unknowns, known unknowns, unknown knowns, and known knowns.

03 · Research opportunities

Three Epistemic States for Design Support

This agenda focuses on the three states in which interaction can change what designers notice, articulate, or reason about. Unknown unknowns call for surfacing an overlooked decision or gap. Known unknowns call for reasoning support, comparison, evidence, expertise, or ways to keep a decision productively open. Unknown knowns call for eliciting or activating knowledge that is already available but not being applied. Known knowns, where the decision and a viable resolution are both clear, are more naturally addressed through execution, acceleration, or automation and are outside my primary research scope.

Cognitive mechanisms are not tied to a single design stage. I treat them as operators on these states: reflection can surface an unknown unknown, comparison or mental simulation can make a known unknown more tractable, externalization and deferred commitment can preserve an unresolved decision, and elicitation or self-explanation can activate an unknown known. The research question is therefore not only which mechanism helps, but when it becomes useful during ongoing CAD work.

Unknown knowns · Knowledge available, not activated

Elicit or activate what is already known

Relevant knowledge, experience, criteria, or evidence are already available to the designer or team, but they have not recognized that these grounds apply to the current decision. Support can cue, externalize, or activate that knowledge.

Working decomposition. I currently treat unknown knowns as at least four distinct forms. This is a research decomposition for generating questions and interactions, not an established four-part taxonomy.

1 · Tacit expertise

The designer knows how to judge or act through accumulated practice, but the knowledge is difficult to verbalize or codify.

Possible mechanisms · elicitation · examples · self-explanation · contrastive questioning

2 · Unarticulated rationale

A decision has been made and a reason may exist, but the rationale remains implicit, incomplete, or unrecorded in the design representation.

Possible mechanisms · in-situ questioning · think-aloud capture · rationale inference · lightweight documentation

3 · Dormant prior knowledge

Relevant knowledge, precedents, earlier decisions, or past discussion exist, but are not retrieved when a new geometry or decision makes them relevant.

Possible mechanisms · retrieval cues · contextual resurfacing · precedent indexing · cross-modal links

4 · Embodied / affective knowledge

The designer's body, perception, intuition, or affective response carries information that may guide judgment before it becomes explicit reasoning.

Possible mechanisms · biofeedback · embodied interaction · reflective visualization · metacognitive monitoring

Research foundation for this decomposition

Tacit expertise: Sawyer, Gervasi & Nuseibeh (2011) explicitly frame unknown knowns as tacit knowledge; Son et al. (CHI 2024) characterize tacit knowledge in professional graphic design; van Kampen (2019) examines making tacit design knowledge explicit in studio education; Ellmers et al. (2026) discuss design expertise as embodied and tacit knowledge.

Unarticulated rationale: ClearFairy (CHI 2026) detects weak or missing explanations, asks in-situ questions, and infers rationale; Lost in Translation (CHI 2026) shows that CAD verbalizations reveal rationale, future actions, and challenges absent from geometry and command history; PointAloud (CHI 2026) supports low-distraction, pointer-anchored think-aloud capture and retrieval.

Dormant prior knowledge: Oxman (1990) models matching present design situations to prior design knowledge; Oxman (1994) organizes design stories for indexed retrieval; Kaleidoscope (CHI 2023) makes design histories visible for reflection; GroundLink (CHI 2026) resurfaces meeting-derived decisions, constraints, and tacit intent inside a 3D editor.

Embodied / affective knowledge: Design with Myself (2024) uses real-time affective biofeedback to support metacognitive monitoring; Ge, Leifer & Shui (2021) study situated emotion in experienced collaborative designers; Ball & Christensen (2019) review design cognition and metacognition, including uncertainty-driven monitoring and control; Cash & Kreye (2018) examine uncertainty perception as a driver of design activity.

Learning, collaboration, and adaptive interaction

Activating existing knowledge. The challenge is not necessarily to provide new information, but to help designers recognize that something they already know is relevant now. Geometry-linked retrieval cues, precedents, self-explanation, or prompts could bring tacit and prior knowledge into the current decision without deciding for the user.

Collaboration. A collaborator may already possess the needed expertise but not realize that it applies to the current geometry. Shared representations can create cues for that expertise to be articulated and attached to the decision.

Adaptation. Once relevant knowledge is activated, the interface should step back or shift support. The state describes a decision at a moment, not a permanent property of the designer.

Known knowns · Resolution is clear

Execute, accelerate, or automate

The decision is recognized and the designer has the knowledge and plan needed to act. This is where automation can efficiently support execution, and it is outside the primary scope of this research agenda.

Unknown unknowns · Gap not recognized

Surface an overlooked decision or knowledge gap

The designer has not recognized a consequential decision or that relevant knowledge is missing. Support first makes the issue visible so it can become something the designer can reason about.

Working decomposition. I currently treat unknown unknowns as at least four recurring forms. This is a research decomposition for generating questions and interactions, not an established four-part taxonomy.

1 · Overlooked requirement / constraint

A relevant requirement, constraint, criterion, or risk is absent from the designer's current problem representation, so it is not yet treated as something that needs reasoning.

Possible mechanisms · reflection · contextual critique · prompting · progressive disclosure

2 · Hidden dependency / consequence

A decision or geometry is visible, but its downstream effects on other components, fabrication, use, or later decisions are not recognized.

Possible mechanisms · dependency tracing · causal reasoning · mental simulation · progressive evaluation

3 · Fixated assumption / narrowed frame

A familiar solution, process, example, or initial framing constrains attention, so alternative formulations or possibilities are not noticed.

Possible mechanisms · reframing · analogy · counterfactual reasoning · defixation

4 · Missing perspective

A relevant user, stakeholder, discipline, or use context is not represented in the current reasoning, leaving its concerns outside the designer's awareness.

Possible mechanisms · perspective taking · multi-perspective critique · collaborative review · role prompting

Research foundation for this decomposition

Overlooked requirement / constraint: ProbLib · Framing Creative Work (C&C 2021) found that novice designers often omit key problem information such as primary stakeholders and obstacles, and used interactive cues to scaffold problem framing.

Hidden dependency / consequence: Nickel, Hurst & Duimering (2024) show that relevant variables, constraints, interdependencies, and trade-offs may remain latent and emerge as design decisions accumulate.

Fixated assumption / narrowed frame: Jansson & Smith (1991) established design fixation as adherence to features of prior examples; Crilly (2015) reviews fixation in professional design practice.

Missing perspective: Surma-aho, Björklund & Hölttä-Otto (2022) characterize perspective taking as a situated design activity and document ways novice teams struggle to form and apply user and stakeholder perspectives.

Learning, collaboration, and adaptive interaction

Recognizing missing grounds. A system might surface an overlooked fabrication constraint, missing test result, or incorrect assumption without immediately resolving the design. Embedded design critics are a precedent for contextual feedback. Geometry-anchored critiques and reflective questions are possible interactions, not established learning outcomes. Here both the decision and the missing grounds may be invisible to the designer.

Collaboration. Multidisciplinary review can surface blind spots and reveal which expertise or evidence is not represented in the current conversation.

Adaptation. An interface could first help a designer recognize the issue, then offer learning resources, a test, or expert input as the need becomes explicit. Inferred understanding is uncertain, so users should be able to dismiss, override, or switch the suggested view.

Known unknowns · Gap recognized

Reason through or keep open a known uncertainty

The designer knows that a consequential decision is unresolved, but cannot yet resolve it confidently. Support can compare alternatives, expose consequences, gather evidence, bring in complementary expertise, or preserve the uncertainty until better grounds become available.

Working decomposition. I currently treat known unknowns as at least four recurring forms. This is a research decomposition for distinguishing what is unresolved and what kind of reasoning support may be useful, not an established four-part taxonomy.

1 · Information / evidence gap

The designer recognizes the decision, but facts, measurements, tests, fabrication knowledge, or expert input needed to resolve it are missing or insufficient.

Possible mechanisms · evidence retrieval · experimentation · expert consultation · learning on demand

2 · Definition / constraint gap

The designer knows that an aspect remains unresolved, but a requirement, target, constraint, parameter, or geometry has not yet been defined precisely enough to commit.

Possible mechanisms · decomposition · constraint articulation · externalization · deferred commitment

3 · Consequence / prediction gap

The designer knows a decision must be made, but cannot confidently anticipate how a choice will affect performance, fabrication, use, or downstream geometry.

Possible mechanisms · mental simulation · analysis · prototyping · progressive evaluation

4 · Alternative / trade-off gap

The designer recognizes plausible alternatives, but their consequential differences, competing criteria, or relative priorities are not yet clear enough for an informed choice.

Possible mechanisms · comparison · juxtaposition · trade-off reasoning · counterfactual exploration

Research foundation for this decomposition

Information / evidence and definition gaps: Wynn, Grebici & Clarkson (2011) describe design uncertainty in terms including lack of knowledge and lack of definition; Aspects of information maturity in design and development (2025) connects these distinctions to how information develops during design.

Consequence / prediction gap: Ball & Christensen (2009) identify mental simulation as a design strategy associated with uncertainty resolution in engineering design meetings.

Alternative / trade-off gap: Nickel, Hurst & Duimering (2024) examine how designers learn about and navigate trade-offs as requirements, constraints, and context evolve; MacLean et al. (1991) use questions, options, and criteria to externalize design rationale and comparison structure.

Learning and collaboration: current considerations

Situated learning. Learning on demand motivates explanations inside an ongoing design activity. Examples, expert input, and guidance that fades could help users work through a known gap. A completed decision is not itself proof of independent learning; test subsequent decision recognition, explanations, and performance after help is removed.

Knowledge across people. A shared CAD model can retain unresolved aspects, constraints, rationale, and partial contributions. Research needs to distinguish whether collaborators resolved a decision or only advanced it, and whether handoffs retain the context needed by the next person.

Design-to-fabrication evidence. An open question is whether support during modeling helps designers understand and apply fabrication knowledge. Possible evidence includes recognizing process constraints, explaining manufacturing choices, revising CAD in response to prototype or fabrication feedback, and reasoning independently in a later task. Physical fabrication feasibility should be examined when a prototype is made, but a successful artifact alone does not demonstrate that its designer learned. This is about reasoning from design to fabrication, not scoring AI-generated geometry.

Comparison and reconsideration. Comparing alternatives, making provisional predictions, and revisiting them when evidence changes can develop decision grounds. Parallel exploration is an interaction opportunity, not proof that the grounds are sufficient or that learning occurred.

Adaptive interfaces: shared model, mechanisms and possible study

Research opportunity. Distinct roles and design tasks may call for different interfaces to the same underlying design. The research opportunity is matching interactions to changing reasoning needs, not simply generating alternative layouts.

Shared representation. One model and data structure might hold geometry, constraints, open decisions, alternatives, rationale, evidence, and provenance. A designer could explore alternatives, an engineer inspect loads, and a physical therapist assess movement using different views of the same decision objects. A single person might also change views as the decision evolves.

Adaptation and continuity. Decision awareness and adequacy of grounds suggest possible triggers for surfacing overlooked choices, comparing alternatives when their grounds are available, offering situated learning, or supporting a handoff when evidence or expertise is missing. These states are research hypotheses, not reliable automatic diagnoses. Suggested views should explain the reason for changing, remain user-controlled, and preserve geometry references, selection, history, and unresolved questions.

Research questions. What evidence indicates a change in reasoning need? What should adapt: information, available operations, modality, or amount of guidance? How do people retain context and decision ownership when switching between specialized views? How can different collaborators maintain shared understanding while seeing different interfaces?

Possible study. Prototype interchangeable views backed by one model and decision schema. Compare a fixed interface, manually chosen task or role views, and context-sensitive suggestions during a multi-stage CAD task or handoff. Examine decision recognition, reasoning, information lost in transitions, handoff accuracy, interruption, and perceived control. This is a proposal, not an implemented system or established finding.

融会贯通 · 中文理解笔记 02

三个象限不是三类 designer，也不是固定的 design stages，而是 decision 会进入和离开的状态

真正重要的是 state transition。同一个 designer 在同一个 session 里，不同 decisions 可以同时处在不同象限；同一个 decision 也会随着新的 geometry、evidence、reflection 或 collaborator input 改变状态。

  • Unknown Unknown → Known Unknown： 一个 reflective prompt、critique 或 dependency cue 先让 designer 意识到“这里其实有个问题”。aCADemy 最接近这种 transition。
  • Unknown Known → explicit reasoning： retrieval cue、self-explanation 或 re-representation 把已经存在但没有被调用的 knowledge 带回当前 decision。它可能随后成为 Known Unknown，也可能直接成为足够的 decision ground。
  • Known Unknown → Known Known： comparison、simulation、test、precedent 或 expert input 让 designer 获得足够 grounds 来 commit。CAMeleon 研究的是这条路径中的 comparison。
  • Known Unknown → remain Known Unknown, productively： 有时候最好的 support 不是马上 resolve，而是把 uncertainty、constraints 和 needed expertise 留在模型里，让团队继续推进而不 premature commitment。FuzzyCAD 就是这一类。

所以我的 agenda 不是“把所有 decision 尽快推到 Known Known”。更准确的是：让 CAD 帮助 designer 面对当前 decision 的 epistemic condition，并支持适合这个 condition 的 reasoning。

融会贯通 · 中文理解笔记 04

探索工具：从一个具体 decision 出发，逐层寻找 research opportunity

这是一套 thinking tool，用来帮助我分析 paper、project、advisor idea 或新的 CAD opportunity。它不作为新的 formal taxonomy。每次都从一个具体 design decision 开始，再看这个 decision 涉及什么 domain、当前处于什么 epistemic state、需要什么 cognitive work，以及 CAD interaction 可以怎样把这个 mechanism 做出来。

Decision
↓

1. What kind of decision is this?

Physical Crafting / Design Thinking / Cognition & Analysis / Ethics & Inclusivity / 其他 domain

↓

2. What is its epistemic state now?

Unknown Unknown / Known Unknown / Unknown Known / Known Known

↓

3. What cognitive work could help?

Reflection / Comparison / Mental Simulation / Reframing / Elicitation / Externalization / Perspective Taking / …

↓

4. What CAD interaction could instantiate that mechanism?

这个顺序把 decision 固定为 unit of analysis。Reasoning domain 帮我扩展 breadth，epistemic state 帮我定位 problem condition，cognitive mechanism 帮我寻找 intervention logic，CAD interaction 则把 mechanism 变成可以设计和研究的 system behavior。

一个很强的 future thread：让已经存在于 CAD 外部的 distributed knowledge 在相关 decision 出现时进入 CAD

TOM 的例子让我更清楚 Unknown Knowns 可以不仅是“designer 自己忘了什么”，也可以是team、Need-Knower、past discussion 或 existing artifacts 中已经存在的 knowledge，没有在当前 CAD context 中被激活。例如 problem description 里已经有身体尺寸、使用场景、活动限制、existing solution 和照片，但 designer 打开 CAD 时这些 knowledge 并没有参与当前 geometry 的 reasoning。

这里的 interaction opportunity 不是简单地“把 brief 放在旁边”，而是研究 contextual retrieval + re-representation + knowledge translation：什么时候把哪一条 information 重新带回来，以及它应该变成文字、dimension、reference geometry、movement envelope、constraint，还是 precedent 3D model。

一个可能的连续研究关系： TOM-context direction 研究 knowledge 如何 enter CAD；FuzzyCAD 研究 unresolved knowledge 如何在 CAD → collaborator → CAD 中被保存和推进。两者合起来可以形成更大的问题：knowledge 如何进入、附着于、并随着 design representation 一起流动。
