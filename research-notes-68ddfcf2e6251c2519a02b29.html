01 · Why the agenda exists · slides 2–16

Support reasoning while designs develop

Computational design tools increasingly generate solutions and make decisions for users. Yet design is a wicked problem: designers may reconsider goals, constraints, evaluation criteria and possibilities as they develop possible solutions. In 3D CAD and digital fabrication, a geometric change can alter material choices and feasible manufacturing workflows, which can in turn reshape the design.

Research question: How can interactive design tools support designers' reasoning as problems and solutions co-evolve?

Scope: 3D CAD and digital fabrication are the current empirical setting. The high-level vision concerns design reasoning and informed decisions, not simply producing a preferred outcome or automating all decisions.

02 · Organizing framework · slides 17–21 and 64

Where reasoning begins × who participates

Problem Space concerns what people want to achieve. Solution Space concerns how to achieve it. The first dimension locates the intervention's primary entry point (problem-first / solution-first). The second asks who participates in developing a design decision: Individual, Group or Community. A location is an analytical emphasis, not a claim that a project never crosses between spaces or participants.

03 · Three complementary studies · slides 22–63

Current research contributions

Individual × Solution-first · slides 22–29

CAMeleon

Research challenge: how to support exploration and comparison of fabrication workflows.

Interaction: compare ways to fabricate an existing CAD model, including workflows that might otherwise be overlooked.

Reported findings: guided comparison expanded exploration, and the discussion shifted from feasibility toward which approach fit a designer's goals. Exploring possible solutions can prompt reconsideration of goals and criteria.

Individual × Problem-first · slides 32–47

aCADemy

Research challenge: help designers recognize and reflect on fabrication considerations during CAD modeling.

Interaction: questions appear in connection with modeling and possible fabrication methods; designers can answer, postpone or respond through design work.

Reported findings: reflection brought fabrication implications into consideration, did not require an immediate answer, and revealed that recognizing a concern is different from knowing how to act.

Group × Solution-first · slides 49–63

FuzzyCAD

Research challenge: precise geometry can make unresolved decisions appear finalized across asynchronous collaboration.

Interaction: mark uncertainty, associate context and constraints with geometry, and hand off unresolved choices.

Reported findings: collaborators contributed domain knowledge without detailed modeling, made progress without finalizing decisions, and sometimes reframed existing problems.

Across studies

The larger reasoning story

CAMeleon explores how alternatives change what is worth considering. aCADemy surfaces fabrication considerations during active design. FuzzyCAD lets reasoning continue across people even when choices are unresolved.

These are different entry points into the co-evolution of design problems and possible solutions.

04 · How interactive tools may help

Mechanisms are a second layer, not new axes

The six-cell framework specifies a reasoning setting (entry point and participation). Interaction mechanisms describe what the tool actually makes possible. A precedent can contain multiple mechanisms and potentially be transferred to more than one cell. The mappings below are working analytical connections, not separate experimentally verified claims.
