The research spine · from the latest A-exam

Design reasoning as problems and solutions co-evolve

The vision is to augment people's ability to reason through complex, open-ended problems and make informed decisions. The organizing questions are where the intervention begins (Problem-first / Solution-first) and who participates (Individual / Group / Community). Cognitive and interaction mechanisms are analyzed within this space, not used as competing framework axes.

How to use Secret Places

从证据出发生成研究机会

  1. Choose a design reasoning situation. Is the initial focus on what to achieve or how to achieve it? Who is participating?
  2. Identify a reasoning difficulty. What needs to be discovered, compared, expressed, revisited, learned or transferred?
  3. Inspect original evidence. Use design theory and the 62 full precedent records. Distinguish the paper's actual interaction from proposed transfer.
  4. Propose an interaction. Choose a mechanism and specify the concrete design action, information and collaborator roles it changes.
  5. State an evaluable question. What evidence would show that the interaction changes reasoning, not merely task completion?

This is a practical ideation workflow for the workspace, not an additional theoretical claim of the A-exam.
