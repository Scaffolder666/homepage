Beyond Design Outcomes

A slide-to-agenda source map for Interactive Tools for Design Reasoning. This page reflects the 81-page PDF provided on October 22, 2026. It replaces the older pre-exam slide planner as the current reference, without erasing that draft.

What the PDF establishes: the research vision, the two organizing dimensions, three project case studies and the explicitly proposed community and longer-term directions. A talk slide is not a substitute for each original research paper or a full evaluation record.

The through-line

Vision → problem

Computational tools can make many solutions. The research focus is what people have opportunities to understand and decide while an open-ended design is developing.

Problem ↔ solution

Designers discover consequences and may reconsider goals, criteria or feasible options. This is the theoretical reason to study the process, not only final design outcomes.

Individual → group

Comparison (CAMeleon), reflection (aCADemy), and unresolved decisions across expertise (FuzzyCAD) provide three concrete investigations of design reasoning.

Group → community

The future questions ask whether reasoning about designs can be transferred, reused, and reconstructed with incomplete evidence in new contexts.

Research mapping rules

Authoritative axis labels: Problem-first / Solution-first × Individual / Group / Community. These classify the primary intervention point, not the cognitive mechanism or an empirical measure.

Secondary coding: comparison, reflection, externalization, retrieval and other interaction techniques help explain how a tool works. Do not substitute prior epistemic-state categories for the current axes.

Evidence discipline: distinguish the source paper's evaluated interaction, the A-exam's summarized finding, your analytical mapping, and any proposed future CAD transfer.
