Research agenda · Work in exploration

Design reasoning as designs evolve

I study how people recognize and reason through consequential design decisions as goals, constraints, and available knowledge evolve. My current setting is CAD, where design choices become embedded in geometry and fabrication plans.

01 · Motivation

Why design decisions need support

A model can look precise even when important decisions remain unsettled. As people model an object, a fabrication choice can affect its geometry, new requirements can challenge an earlier judgment, and questions may arise that require expertise outside the designer's own field.

I investigate how interactive design tools can support the reasoning surrounding these decisions: bringing relevant issues to attention, making alternatives and assumptions discussable, and allowing work to progress without treating every choice as final.

02 · Conceptual framing

Awareness and ability to resolve

I use a particular person, design decision, and moment in time as a starting point for examining design reasoning. Decision awareness asks whether someone recognizes that a decision needs attention. Ability to resolve asks whether they have sufficient knowledge and resources to reason through that decision. Ability is not the same as confidence or a final commitment.

These two dimensions suggest four situations. They are a conceptual lens for generating and investigating research questions, not a validated classification of every design activity. In collaboration, different people can occupy different situations for the same decision; the quadrants do not assign an entire team a single state.

Design problems evolve

Rittel & Webber (1973) and Dorst & Cross (2001) motivate examining how problem formulations and possible solutions change together.

Knowledge is situated and distributed

Ball & Christensen (2019) provide a lens on metacognition; Star & Griesemer (1989) inform questions about working across different perspectives.

05 · Cross-cutting questions

When decisions and people change

Awareness and ability are not fixed stages. A decision may change for one designer over time and may be understood differently across collaborators. These questions connect the four situations.

Decision transitions

RQ17How might CAD tools adapt their support as a decision shifts from unnoticed to unresolved or ready for commitment?

Distributed expertise

RQ18How might CAD tools direct unresolved decisions to collaborators with complementary expertise without losing design context?

Learning transfer

RQ19How might support for fabrication decisions in one CAD task help designers reason independently in later tasks?

Human–AI agency

RQ20How might AI-assisted CAD adapt reflective guidance to designers' evolving understanding while preserving decision ownership?

06 · Looking ahead

Research trajectory

These questions motivate research on learning over time, coordination across expertise, and how forms of reasoning support vary beyond CAD. I am developing the next studies and will add more precise research designs, evaluation measures, and boundaries as they are established.

Next study design · In development. An immediate challenge is distinguishing whether a decision was genuinely overlooked, deliberately deferred, or recognized without enough knowledge to act. Future study plans will make these distinctions and their evaluation criteria explicit.

Broader scope · Open. Which decision challenges transfer to other design practices, and which require domain-specific tools and expertise?
