Research agenda · Work in exploration

Design reasoning as designs evolve

I study how people reason through design decisions as goals, constraints, and available knowledge evolve. My current work explores these questions in CAD.

Design decision space

Two dimensions guide this agenda: awareness of a decision and the ability to resolve it. Both describe a person’s current situation in relation to a specific decision and can change during design. The ability to resolve a decision depends on whether the designer has sufficient knowledge and resources to reason through it, even if the final choice remains open.

Not aware · Can resolve

Discover a decision

A designer has relevant expertise but has not noticed that a decision needs attention.

Open question

Why do designers overlook decisions they have the expertise to address, and what might help them recognize these decisions during design?

Related theory and research

Situated invention · Suwa et al. (2000) — Unexpected features noticed in sketches can contribute to the formation of new design issues and requirements.

Kinds of seeing · Schön & Wiggins (1992) — Designers perceive and judge visual and spatial qualities while reasoning through changes to their designs.

Design fixation · Crilly (2015) — Professional designers can become constrained by existing solutions and reflect on strategies for overcoming that fixation.

More design research (9)

Visual re-cognition · Oxman (2002) — Visual and conceptual reinterpretation can make new forms and meanings emerge from a design.

Opportunistic design · Guindon (1990) — New requirements and partial solutions can prompt designers to move between levels of abstraction rather than follow a fixed sequence.

Visual representations as epistemic objects · Ewenstein & Whyte (2009) — In an architectural design study, incomplete visual representations supported ongoing inquiry and knowledge development.

Design fixation (original study) · Jansson & Smith (1991) — Designers shown an example solution reproduced its features, including flawed ones, even when warned against them.

Reflection-in-action · Schön (1983) — Practitioners notice surprises in a situation’s response and reframe the problem while still acting on it.

Seeing-as and seeing-that · Goldschmidt (1991) — Sketching alternates between interpretive and descriptive ways of looking at an emerging form.

Perception in design sketches · Suwa & Tversky (1997) — Architects and students attended to different kinds of relationships when reviewing their own sketches.

Expertise in design · Cross (2004) — A review of how experienced and novice designers differ in framing and structuring design problems.

Ambiguity as a resource · Gaver, Beaver & Benford (2003) — Deliberate ambiguity can prompt people to interpret a design and form their own account of it.

Aware · Can resolve

Reason through a decision

A designer recognizes a decision and has the relevant knowledge to address it. Evaluating alternatives, weighing trade-offs, and making a judgment remain part of the design work.

Open question

How can design tools support comparison and judgment while leaving room to explore alternatives?

Related theory and research

Design rationale / QOC · MacLean et al. (1991) — Questions, options, and criteria provide a structure for examining design alternatives and their reasoning.

Prior knowledge in design · Oxman (1990) — Designers can adapt, restructure, and reformulate precedents to use past knowledge in new situations.

Constructive memory · Liew & Gero (2004) — This computational design model reconstructs memories of past experience according to the current design situation.

More design research (12)

Design stories · Oxman (1994) — A model links issues, concepts, and forms so design precedents can be explored across contexts.

Primary generator · Darke (1979) — An initial guiding idea or constraint can organize the development and testing of a design.

Analogical reasoning and mental simulation · Ball & Christensen (2009) — Designers use analogies and imagined consequences as reasoning strategies associated with uncertainty.

Briefing and reframing · Paton & Dorst (2011) — Experienced designers reinterpret a client's framing and communicate alternative ways of understanding a problem.

Parallel prototyping · Dow et al. (2010) — Creating and evaluating multiple prototypes in parallel produced better results and more divergence than serial iteration.

Subjunctive interfaces · Lunzer & Hornbæk (2008) — Interfaces that let people set up, view, and control alternative scenarios side by side.

Side views · Terry & Mynatt (2002) — On-demand previews show what a command would produce before committing to it.

Variation in element and action · Terry et al. (2004) — Tool support for developing several alternative solutions at the same time.

Design as exploration · Hartmann et al. (2008) — Parallel authoring and runtime tuning let designers compare interface alternatives directly.

gIBIS · Conklin & Begeman (1988) — A hypertext system recording issues, positions, and arguments during design discussion; an antecedent of QOC.

Satisficing · Simon (1996) — Under bounded rationality, designers accept an option that is good enough rather than search for an optimum.

Abduction in design · Dorst (2011) — Design reasoning is characterised as proposing a working principle and an intended outcome together.

Not aware · Cannot resolve

Recognize a knowledge need

A designer has not recognized a consequential decision and lacks some of the knowledge needed to resolve it.

Open question

How can designers recognize a decision when they do not yet know what knowledge they are missing?

Related theory and research

Design knowledge needs · Ahmed & Wallace (2004) — An empirical study found that novice engineering designers often did not explicitly recognize their own knowledge needs.

Design metacognition · Ball & Christensen (2019) — A review describes how uncertainty drives monitoring and regulation of design thinking.

More design research (7)

Design risks framework · Carlson et al. (2020) — Identifies metacognitive processes for focusing attention, recognizing risks, and choosing iteration strategies; novice teams lacked some risk-identification knowledge.

Embedded design critics · Fischer et al. (1993) — Contextual critiques in design environments can surface problems and prompt reflection during design.

Knowing what is not known · Miyake & Norman (1979) — Asking a question requires enough knowledge to locate the gap, so questions depend on what the learner already knows.

Metacognition and cognitive monitoring · Flavell (1979) — The original account of metacognitive knowledge and of monitoring one’s own understanding.

Paradox of the active user · Carroll & Rosson (1987) — People prefer to act rather than consult instructions, so gaps in their knowledge can persist unnoticed.

The role of critiquing · Fischer et al. (1991) — Critics embedded in a design environment present relevant knowledge at the moment the work makes it applicable.

Inventing to prepare for future learning · Schwartz & Martin (2004) — Attempting a problem before instruction helped students recognise what the instruction addressed.

Aware · Cannot resolve

Build understanding or seek input

A designer recognizes a decision but still needs relevant knowledge, evidence, or expertise. Learning and contributions from others offer different ways to move it forward.

Open question

How can unresolved decisions receive relevant input and be refined as a design develops?

Related theory and research

Symmetry of ignorance · Fischer (2000) — Complex design knowledge is distributed across people, making differences in expertise a resource for collaborative creativity.

Boundary objects · Star & Griesemer (1989) — Shared objects can accommodate different viewpoints while retaining a recognizable identity across groups.

Boundary negotiating artifacts · Lee (2007) — Artifacts in collaboration can help establish, reshape, and contest the boundaries and practices of joint work.

More design research (10)

Knowledge boundaries · Carlile (2004) — Crossing syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic boundaries may require transferring, translating, or transforming knowledge.

Collaborative reframing · Stompff et al. (2016) — Surprises in multidisciplinary teams can prompt shared sensemaking and new problem frames.

Disciplinary difference and uncertainty · Dyer et al. (2021) — Design disciplines can understand and value uncertainty differently, with implications for interdisciplinary work.

Micro-conflicts and uncertainty · Paletz et al. (2017) — Brief disagreements were followed by different changes in uncertainty across the design teams studied.

Delaying decisions · Ward, Liker, Cristiano & Sobek (1995) — Keeping several options open and narrowing them gradually is described as speeding development rather than slowing it.

Object worlds · Bucciarelli (1994) — An ethnography of engineering design in which participants work within different disciplinary worlds and negotiate across them.

Visual representations in engineering · Henderson (1999) — A study of how drawings and CAD systems shape what engineers can share, contest, and negotiate.

Shared understanding in co-design · Kleinsmann & Valkenburg (2008) — Barriers and enablers for shared understanding identified at the actor, project, and company levels.

Teamwork and social processes · Cross & Cross (1995) — An observational study of how a design team coordinated roles, disagreement, and shared understanding.

Situated learning · Lave & Wenger (1991) — Learning is described as increasing participation in a community of practice rather than transfer of information.

Across the decision space

Problem–solution co-evolution · Dorst & Cross (2001) — Design problems and possible solutions develop together, changing which decisions matter.

Situated FBS · Gero & Kannengiesser (2004) — Designing is shaped by recursive relationships between changing environments, interpretations, and expectations.

Co-evolution and emergence · Dorst (2019) — Exploring solutions can lead designers to reinterpret the design problem itself.

Expansive learning for collaborative design · Tessier (2022) — Team challenges and actions can become material for collective reflection and learning during design.

Learning on demand · Fischer (1996) — Design environments can connect learning to the knowledge needed in an ongoing task.

Wicked problems · Rittel & Webber (1973) — Planning problems have no definitive formulation or stopping rule, and each attempt at a solution changes the problem.

Co-evolution formalised · Maher, Poon & Boulanger (1996) — A computational model in which the problem space and the solution space evolve in response to each other.

Structure of design problem spaces · Goel & Pirolli (1992) — A comparison across design disciplines identifying features they share as problem-solving activity.

C-K design theory · Hatchuel & Weil (2009) — Designing is modelled as expansion between a space of concepts and a space of knowledge.

Cognitive artifacts of designing · Visser (2006) — A synthesis of cognitive design research framing designing as the construction of representations.

Designerly ways of knowing · Cross (2006) — A collection arguing that design has its own forms of knowledge and enquiry.

Questions beyond the current setting

Learning may change which decisions a designer can recognize or resolve over time, while collaborators with different expertise may face different states for the same decision. I am also interested in how these questions arise in design domains beyond CAD.
